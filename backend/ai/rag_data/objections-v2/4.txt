Objection: “I am not sure this will work for me”
1. Clarify what the prospect means
SCRIPT
“Oh, in what way?”

WHY

The objection can mean different things. The prospect may be uncertain about:
- You and whether you can deliver what you promised.
- Your product, service, or solution.
- Themselves and whether they can use it or succeed with it.
- In rare circumstances, it may be a smoke screen.
The transcript emphasizes that assuming you already know what the prospect means puts you at a disadvantage.

HOW

Ask the short clarifying question rather than guessing. Let the prospect explain why they feel it will not work for them.

2. Build certainty by asking what would prevent it from working
SCRIPT
“Oh, what would what would cause it not to work for you since it's already working for companies in your industry right now?”

WHY

The question shifts the frame toward the fact that the solution is already working for others in the prospect’s industry. Instead of trying to prove the solution, the seller asks the prospect why it would not work for them.

HOW

Adapt the question to the product or service. For a consumer offer, the transcript gives this example:
“Oh, what would I guess what would cause it to not work for you since it's already working for people that have this illness that you have?”

Use only a comparison that is relevant to the prospect’s actual situation.

3. Build the prospect into the identity of a buyer
SCRIPT
“I need to build them into the identity of the prospect who becomes a buyer.”

WHY

The transcript explains that people make decisions based on the identity of who they believe they are. If a prospect believes they never make decisions on the spot, the seller needs to build them into the identity of someone who makes quick and smart decisions.

HOW

The transcript says to build this identity throughout the conversation, from the first words to the close. It does not provide a single universal script for doing this; the framing needs to be adapted to the prospect and industry.

4. Use a playful question if they take too long to answer
SCRIPT
“Hold on, maybe it wouldn't work for you because of your hair. I mean, you do have blonde hair. It only works for people of like purple or like pink hair, right? Is it your hair?”

WHY

The speaker says the playful question can free the prospect up and help them let their guard down. The prospect may realize that the reason it would not work for them is not obvious.

HOW

Use this only when you can see the prospect and read their body language, and they take more than a couple of seconds to think. Deliver it playfully. The transcript emphasizes that tone communicates the intention behind what you say.

5. If the concern is time, clarify how much time they think they need
SCRIPT
“Oh, well, how much time do you think you would need?”

WHY

The prospect may be objecting to a time commitment they have assumed, rather than the actual time required. The transcript gives the example of a prospect assuming they need two hours a day.

HOW

Ask what amount of time they believe they need. Then respond to the amount they give you, rather than assuming you already know their concern.

6. Connect the time commitment to their desired result
SCRIPT
“Would you be able to put 20 to 30 minutes a day in to to master Mandarin? So when you do meet your new in-laws, they actually like you can actually talk to them.”

WHY

The transcript says to focus on the end result the prospect wants, not merely on having time to go through a course.

HOW

Repeat the prospect’s stated desired outcome while discussing the time commitment. In this example, the prospect wants to learn Mandarin so they can communicate with their future in-laws.

7. Use an identity frame for a prospect who values their time
SCRIPT
“And that's why I love working with department heads like you because you know that once the time is gone, you can never get it back. So department heads like you, they're always looking for services that they can implement and systems they can implement to automate it. So it actually frees up more of their time. Is that why you came to us?”

WHY

The transcript describes this as building the prospect into the identity of a smart, forward-thinking department head who makes hard decisions and knows that systems can free up time.

HOW

Use this framing when the prospect is a department head and the context supports the idea that they value their time and are looking for ways to automate work.

8. Use a playful tone to help the prospect relax
SCRIPT
“Oh, why would he even say that? Of course, it's going to work for me.”

WHY

The transcript presents this as the prospect’s possible internal reaction to the seller’s playful question. The speaker says that getting someone to laugh can help their nervous system calm down and relax.

HOW

Use a playful tone strategically, rather than sounding like you are trying to prove the solution. The transcript emphasizes that the prospect interprets your intention through your tone.
