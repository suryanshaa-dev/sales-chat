Objection: “I need to talk to my spouse”
1. Prevent the objection through pre-framing
SCRIPT

How does your spouse feel about you...

Repeat back the end result that they want.

WHY

Top 1% salespeople weren't necessarily focusing on the latest, greatest rebuttal to an objection. They were more concerned with preventing the objection from happening in the prospect's mind.

This is called objection prevention.

You want to pre-frame at the beginning of a sales conversation to help prevent the objection of “I just need to talk with my spouse” from even happening in the prospect's mind.

HOW
- Ask the question somewhere between 25% and 50% into the conversation.
- Do not ask it in the first two minutes.
- Do not wait until the very end unless you need to ask it again.
- Ask how the spouse feels about the end result the prospect wants.
- Repeat back the end result or the pain they feel from the problem.

2. Repeat back the specific end result
SCRIPT

How does your spouse feel about you losing the 108 pounds so you're able to walk your daughter down the aisle in 10 to 15 years?

WHY

You're repeating back what they want, losing the weight because of the end result they want.

It's hard for that prospect to say their spouse wouldn't want them to do that.

This is how you're starting to pre-frame where they don't necessarily need to go and talk with the spouse.

HOW
- Find out specifically what the prospect wants to achieve.
- Ask why that result is so important to them.
- Repeat back the result they told you.
- Ask how their spouse feels about them achieving that result.
- Do not introduce a reason they didn't tell you.
Example from the transcript

The prospect wants to lose 108 pounds so they can walk their daughter down the aisle.

The question connects losing the weight to that specific result.

3. Use “feel” instead of “think”
SCRIPT

How does your spouse feel about you having enough coverage so she can pay off the house and all the expenses when you pass away, so she doesn't have to get a second job?

WHY

Feel keeps them on their emotional side of the brain, whereas think puts them back over into their logical side of the brain.

HOW
- Say “How does your spouse feel about...”
- Do not say “What does your spouse think about...”
- Repeat back the result they said they wanted.
- Connect the result to the reason they gave you for wanting it.

4. Repeat back the negative consequence if nothing changes
SCRIPT

I mean, does she want you to lose the 105 pounds so you don't keep putting all that pressure on your heart and possibly have a heart attack?

WHY

You're repeating back the end result they said they wanted.

You're also repeating back the consequence if they don't change.

This helps the prospect think about the negative consequences if they don't change their situation.

HOW
- Repeat back the end result they said they wanted.
- Identify the negative consequence if nothing changes.
- Ask whether their spouse wants them to achieve that result or avoid that consequence.
- Use the consequence only if the prospect has already told you about it.

5. If the prospect says they are not sure what their spouse would say
SCRIPT

I mean, does she want you to...

Well, would he want you to be able to...

Repeat back either the end result or the negative consequence if nothing changes.

WHY

Some prospects will come back with:

“I'm not really sure. I'd have to ask them.”

You can't just be like okay, because you might get that objection at the end.

HOW
- Ask whether their spouse wants them to achieve the result.
- Repeat back the end result they want.
- Alternatively, repeat back the negative consequence if nothing changes.

6. Use an identity frame
SCRIPT

Oh, well, good for you both.

I mean, good for the both of you. You'd be surprised. I talk to a lot of people, and some of them, you know, they just keep pushing it down the road. They never end up losing the weight, and then, you know, guess what ends up happening to them?

WHY

This is called an NEPQ identity frame.

You help the prospect identify and basically tell themselves why they will want to solve this problem.

You help them identify why they and their spouse will want to solve the problem and actually do something.

HOW
- Say “good for you both.”
- Include both the prospect and their spouse.
- Use a concerned tone.
- Explain that some people keep pushing it down the road.
- Ask what ends up happening to them.
- Let the prospect think about the negative consequences.

7. Use a concerned tone
WHY

Your tone is how the prospect interprets the intention behind what you're saying and asking.

If they feel you're genuinely concerned, they open up.

If you sound like you're reading a sales script, it sounds unnatural and like you're trying to manipulate them.

HOW
- Use a concerned tone.
- Use natural pauses.
- Avoid sounding like you're reading a script.
- Let your facial expression and tone communicate genuine concern.

8. Include the spouse in the decision
SCRIPT

I mean, for you and your spouse, why is doing this so important to you now?

WHY

You're including the spouse in every question.

The prospect is thinking about why they and their spouse want to solve the problem.

HOW
- Include the spouse in the question.
- Repeat back what the prospect said they wanted.
- Ask why doing this is so important to them now.
- Focus on the result they want or the timing of why they want it now.

9. Use the question to focus on timing
SCRIPT

For you and your spouse, starting your own business, why is this so important to you now, though?

WHY

If you're focused on “now, though,” it's a timing thing.

If you're focused on “this,” it's why they're wanting to start their own business.

HOW
- Include the spouse in the question.
- Focus on “now, though” when asking about timing.
- Focus on “this” when asking why they want to achieve the result.
- Repeat back what they said they wanted.

10. Use the technique throughout the conversation
WHY

You want to pre-frame much better in your sales conversations to help prevent the spouse objection from even happening in their mind.

The transcript emphasizes that you should build trust before using these questions.

HOW
- Ask the questions approximately 25% to 50% of the way into the conversation.
- Build a gap and have trust before using the questions.
- Use the technique in person, virtually, or on the phone.
- Apply the prospect's own stated reasons and desired outcomes.
- Use the questions to help prevent the spouse objection, rather than relying only on rebuttals at the end.
