Objection: “I am too busy”
1. Agree and deframe the prospect
SCRIPT
“Oh yeah, and I've only got a few minutes before my next appointment anyways.”

WHY

The transcript explains that prospects may feel they are being sold to and want to get rid of the salesperson. Agreeing and indicating that you also have limited time can disarm them.

The speaker calls this a deframe: getting the prospect out of their current frame of thinking.

HOW

Agree first. The transcript says that if the prospect feels you will take only a little of their time, their guard is more likely to go down than if they expect a 30-minute conversation.

2. Reframe the conversation around whether you can help
SCRIPT
“And just so you know I'm not quite sure we can even help you yet it might not even make sense for you to even have us call you back I'd have to understand probably a little bit more about kind of what you guys are using now for XYZ and the results you're getting from that because maybe you don't even need it”

WHY

The seller is not claiming they cannot help. They are staying neutral because they do not yet know enough about the prospect.

The transcript explains that the goal is to lower the prospect’s guard, then understand their situation and build a gap between where they are and where they want to be.

HOW

Do not stop after agreeing. Continue by explaining that you need to understand their current situation and results before knowing whether your solution makes sense.

3. Move directly into a situation question
SCRIPT
“Who do you guys use for”

WHY

This moves the conversation away from the prospect’s objection and into understanding their current situation.

HOW

The transcript says to go directly into the first NEPQ situation question. Adapt the question to what you sell and what you need to understand.

4. Use a softer, more serious tone
SCRIPT
“What do you use now for”

WHY

The transcript says leaning in and using a softer tone can make the question sound more serious and help the prospect open up.

HOW

Lean in when asking the situation question. The speaker describes this as making the tone sound softer and more serious.

5. If they say they are busy at the end, schedule a specific time
SCRIPT
“Well yeah it might be hard to randomly get a hold of me like that with my schedule uh what I can do if if it helps you you have your calendar handy I can pull up mine and have you book a specific time with me that way you don't have to chase me down and vice versa would that help you”

WHY

The transcript says this helps the prospect feel that you are about to leave rather than stay and keep selling. It also presents you as an expert with a busy schedule, rather than someone available at all times.

HOW

Use this when the prospect says they will get back to you if interested. Have them book a specific time instead of relying on a vague callback.

6. Ask what they want to go over in their mind
SCRIPT
“Hey before I jump off the phone or before I jump off Zoom what was it that you were I guess wanting to go over in your mind just so I know what questions you'll have when we talk on Thursday”

WHY

The transcript says this wording is less defensive than directly asking what they are unsure of or what is preventing them from moving forward.

HOW

Ask this after arranging the next appointment, when the prospect feels you are about to leave. Replace the day with the actual scheduled follow-up.

7. Ask what is really holding them back
SCRIPT
“Hey before I go and this is you know this is between me and you it's it's off the record I'm not going to go post it on on Facebook what's really I guess holding you back from moving forward so you're able to XYZ”

WHY

The transcript presents this as another way to uncover the prospect’s real concern. It frames the conversation as collaborative rather than adversarial.

HOW

Use a playful tone for the “not going to go post it” part, then repeat the outcome the prospect said they wanted in place of “XYZ.”
