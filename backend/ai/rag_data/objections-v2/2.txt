Objection: “It's too expensive”
1. Agree and shift to results-based thinking
SCRIPT

Yeah, that's not a problem. Um, tell me if you did have the funds, is this something that would work for you? Curious tone.

WHY

First of all, you just agree with them to get them to let their guard down.

You want to get your prospects into results-based thinking quickly versus price or cost-based thinking.

You want them thinking about results, not budget.

HOW
- Agree with the prospect.
- Say “that's not a problem” slowly.
- Use a curious tone.
- Verbal pause and pace the question.
- Ask whether the solution would work for them if they did have the funds.
- Do not immediately start talking about price or defending your price.

2. Use “funds” or “funding” instead of “budget”
SCRIPT

Tell me if you did have the funding, is this something that would work for you?

WHY

Budget is limited. It is a capped way of thinking.

Funds or funding is not necessarily capped.

You don't want your prospects thinking they have to be at a cap. You want them to think results-based thinking, not budget thinking.

HOW
- If you're selling B2C, say “funds.”
- If you're selling B2B, say “funding.”
- Do not say “budget.”
- Use “funds” or “funding” to avoid creating a capped way of thinking.

3. Acknowledge the money concern and ask how they can resolve it
SCRIPT

Yeah, and I can appreciate that money might be an issue. From what you told me, I guess, how do you feel you can resolve that so you can find the funds so that you can...

WHY

You want your prospects to stay on their emotional side of the brain because that's how they make buying decisions.

If you say “think,” where would their brain go? Think is logical brain thinking.

You want them to stay in emotional thinking.

You're tying in the funds and them getting the funding to getting what they said they wanted.

If they can get the funding, they get what they want. If they can't get the funds, they don't get what they want.

HOW
- Acknowledge that money might be an issue.
- Refer back to what the prospect told you.
- Ask how they feel they can resolve that.
- Use “feel” instead of “think.”
- Repeat back what they said they wanted.
- Tie finding the funds to getting the result they said they wanted.
- Pay attention to your tonality and body language.

4. Tie the funding to the result they want
SCRIPT

How do you feel you can resolve that so you can find the funds so that you can...

WHY

You're tying in the funds and them getting the funding to getting what they said they wanted.

If they can get the funding, they get what they want. If they can't get the funds, they don't get what they want.

You're tying that into the result they said they wanted.

HOW
- Ask how they feel they can resolve the money issue.
- Repeat back what they said they wanted.
- Connect the funds to the result they want.
- Let the prospect explain how they could find the funds.

5. Explore possible ways to find the funds
WHY

The prospect may have other ways to find the funds.

The transcript explains that prospects may be able to use a credit card, get a loan, shift money from something else they are currently spending, or move funding from another department.

HOW

Listen to what the prospect says about finding the funds.

Examples from the transcript:
- “I could use a credit card.”
- “Maybe I could get a loan.”
- Shift the money from something else they were currently spending that on over to your solution.
- If you sell B2B, move the funds from a different department budget to your solution to buy it.

6. Prevent the price objection through results-based thinking
WHY

If you hate competing on price and don't like being commoditized, you're going to want to get your prospects into results-based thinking quickly versus price or cost-based thinking.

You have to do that from the very first words and questions out of your mouth.

HOW
- Start the conversation with questions that focus on results.
- Avoid price or cost-based thinking.
- Use “funds” or “funding” instead of “budget.”
- Tie finding the funds to getting the result they said they wanted.
- Use specific skilled questions throughout the conversation, from the start to the close of your sales process.
