Objection: “I just need some time to think it over”
1. Agree and establish a timeframe
SCRIPT

Yeah, that's not a problem. What's your time frame on getting back to me in the next day or so, just to see if I'll be available for you?

WHY

The first job is to get the prospect to let their guard down. If you cannot get them to let their guard down, they are not going to tell you what the real concern actually is.

Asking directly what they need to think about can make the prospect defensive and cause them to throw out more objections.

Saying “just to see if I'd be available for you” raises your status in their mind.

HOW
- Agree with the prospect instead of challenging their objection.
- Ask for a timeframe on getting back to you.
- Use the phrase “just to see if I'd be available for you.”
- Do not immediately ask what they need to think about.

2. Create a calendar commitment
SCRIPT

Well, yeah, I mean, I'm not sure if I'd be randomly available like that with my schedule. What I can do though, if it helps you, is if you have your calendar handy, I can pull up mine and have you book a specific time with me. That way you don't have to chase me down and vice versa. Would that help you if I did that?

WHY

You cannot have a prospect where you're just waiting on them calling you back.

If you do that, 99% never call you back.

You want to raise your status by positioning yourself as someone who is busy and has lots of clients.

The calendar commitment creates a firm time instead of relying on a vague promise.

HOW
- If the prospect does not give you a specific time, explain that you may not be randomly available.
- Ask them to have their calendar handy.
- Pull up your own calendar.
- Have them book a specific time.
- Explain that they will not have to chase you down and vice versa.
- Ask: “Would that help you if I did that?”

3. Ask before leaving
SCRIPT

Now John, before I go, I guess, what were you wanting to go over in your mind, that way I know what questions you'll have when we talk tomorrow?

WHY

The biggest reason for booking another appointment is that now their guard is down.

They think you're going to leave.

If you're on the phone, they think you're about to get off the phone. If you're on Zoom, they think you're going to get off Zoom.

They don't feel any sales pressure because they feel like you're going to leave.

This is where the magic begins.

HOW
- Wait until the appointment is booked.
- Before leaving, ask what they wanted to go over in their mind.
- Connect the question to the next conversation.
- Say that you want to know what questions they'll have when you talk tomorrow.
- Do not ask directly: “What do you need to think about?”

4. Use verbal pacing
SCRIPT

Now Sally, before I go, I guess, what were you wanting to go over in your mind, so I know what questions you'll have when we talk on Tuesday?

WHY

You have to verbal pace this out.

Your tone is how your prospect interprets why you're asking the question.

A curious tone causes them to actually open up and tell you what they wanted to go over in their mind.

HOW
- Use a curious tone.
- Shift your facial expression into a curious expression.
- Slow down the delivery.
- Avoid sounding confrontational or demanding.
- Let the prospect respond.

5. Use the question to uncover the real objection
SCRIPT

What were you wanting to go over in your mind, just so I know what questions you'll have when we talk tomorrow?

WHY

“I want to think it over” is a defensive mechanism in their mind where they don't want to tell you what the real objection is.

The real objection is what they want to think about.

The prospect may reveal that the actual concern is money, a spouse, or time.

HOW

Ask the question with a curious tone and listen to what they say.

Examples from the transcript:
- Money objection: “It's a big decision and I'm just not sure if we have the money or budget for it.”
- Spouse objection: “I really need to talk with my husband. He's not going to be here till tonight.”
- Time objection: “I just don't know if I have time to really do this right now.”
Once you find out the real objection, you can help them overcome it.
